How isol8 is different

A team of safe, cloud-hosted AI agents that anyone can run — versus every other way people use agents today.

Most ways to “use AI agents” right now fall into a few buckets: running an agent on your own machine, chatting with a single assistant, building rigid automations, or coding your own framework. Here is how isol8 compares.

In short, isol8 is the only option that is cloud-hosted, sandboxed, always-on, a team you orchestrate, and one click for non-technical people — all at once.

isol8 vs running agents on your own machine

Running an agent locally — OpenClaw, AutoGPT, or a desktop AI app — gives it access to your whole computer, takes technical setup to do safely, and stops the moment you close your laptop.

isol8 runs the same kind of agents in an isolated cloud pod instead: one-click setup, sandboxed so they only touch what you connect, and always-on so they keep working around the clock. You get the power of a local agent without the risk or the babysitting.

isol8 vs a single AI assistant

A single assistant (the ChatGPT or Lindy model) is one agent you chat with. It is great for answering and small tasks, but you are still the one coordinating everything.

isol8 gives you a whole team of agents led by Friday, your orchestrator. You describe one goal and Friday delegates it across specialists, then brings back the result. It is the difference between one helper and a staffed back office.

isol8 vs workflow automation (Zapier, Make, n8n)

Automation tools make you build rigid trigger-and-action flows by hand, and they break when reality does not match the flowchart.

isol8 is agentic: you describe what you want in plain language and the agents figure out the steps themselves, adapting as things change. No flows to wire up, no maintenance when a step changes.

isol8 vs agent frameworks (LangChain, CrewAI, AutoGPT)

Frameworks are code you write and infrastructure you host yourself. They are powerful, but they are built for developers.

isol8 is the hosted, no-code version: the harness, the sandbox, the orchestration, and the storage all come out of the box. Non-technical people get the same capability without writing or running anything.

01Pods & your container

Your private, always-on home in the cloud, where your agents live and work.

When you subscribe, isol8 spins up a container that is yours alone — a small, isolated cloud machine running the agent runtime. We call it your pod. Every agent you have (starting with Friday and Atlas) runs inside this one pod, not as separate machines.

It stays on around the clock, so your agents keep working even when you are offline. There is no scale-to-zero while your subscription is active.

02Cloud vs local

Why isol8 runs your agents in the cloud instead of on your own computer.

A capable agent running on your laptop can, in principle, reach your whole machine — your files, your apps, your credentials. Setting one up safely is a technical job, and it stops the moment you close the lid.

isol8 runs your agents in their own cloud pod instead. Nothing installs on your computer, they only reach what you explicitly connect, and they run 24/7. You trade “on my machine” for safe, always-on, and zero-setup.

03The sandbox

The wall around your pod that keeps agents contained and least-privilege.

Your pod is sandboxed: it runs in its own isolated cloud environment, walled off from everyone else’s and from your own devices. An agent can only touch the specific tools, files, and integrations you connect to it.

So even if a model hallucinates or misfires, the blast radius is limited to what you handed it — it can’t delete everything or reach sensitive info you never gave it. Least-privilege is the default, not something you configure.

04ECS & EFS

The AWS building blocks under every pod: the compute and the storage.

  • ECS (Elastic Container Service) on Fargate is the AWS service that actually runs your pod’s container — serverless compute, no servers for you to manage. Each user gets their own ECS service.
  • EFS (Elastic File System) is the persistent storage attached to your pod. Your workspace lives at /mnt/efs/users/<you>/, with a separate folder per agent (agents/<agent_id>/). It survives restarts, so agents keep their files, memory, and history.

In short: ECS is the engine, EFS is the hard drive. The pod is the isolation boundary; agents are separated within it by their own workspace folders.

in the stack · per-user ECS Fargate service + per-user EFS access point

05The agent harness

The runtime that turns a model into an agent that can actually do things.

A raw language model just produces text. The agent harness is what wraps it into a working agent: it runs the think→act loop, calls tools, manages memory, and loads skills and plugins. isol8 runs an extended build of the open-source OpenClaw runtime as this harness.

So when we say “an agent,” we mean a configured identity (its personality and instructions) running on this harness inside your pod — not a separate app or model.

06Friday & Atlas

The two agents every pod starts with: your orchestrator and your recruiter.

Friday is your AI chief of staff — the one agent you talk to, who breaks down your goals and delegates work across the rest of your team. Atlas is your in-house recruiter, who helps you build and onboard new agents. Both are seeded automatically when your pod is provisioned.

07Paperclip

The “company” layer that holds your agents, their threads, and how they work together.

Under the hood, your agents aren’t loose processes — they’re organized into a company structure powered by a per-tenant service called Paperclip. It’s the backbone of the Teams experience: agents, threads, channels, members, and the messages between them.

When your pod is set up, isol8 creates your Paperclip company and seeds Friday and Atlas into it. It’s what makes a team of agents possible, rather than a pile of separate bots.

08Orchestration

Talk to one agent; it coordinates the rest.

You don’t manage a dozen agents or wire up a workflow. You give Friday a single goal, and Friday — the orchestrator — splits it into pieces, hands each to the right agent on your team, and assembles the result back for you.

That delegation is the difference between “a chatbot” and “a team that gets a job done.”

09The marketplace

A storefront of ready-made agents you can hire into your pod in one click.

Anyone can build a specialized agent — a personal trainer, a bookkeeper, a grant writer — and publish it to the isol8 marketplace. You browse, and deploy a copy straight into your own pod. Some are free; some cost a few dollars, set by the builder.

It’s how specialization becomes one click instead of a build project.

10Publish & deploy

How an agent goes from a seller’s pod to a buyer’s pod, safely.

  • Publishing: a seller snapshots their agent into an immutable, versioned artifact (a tarball of its workspace + a manifest + a config slice), stored in S3. It’s scanned for leaked credentials and PII, then moves draft → review → published after an admin approves. Old versions stay frozen, so nothing breaks under existing buyers.
  • Deploying: when you hire a listing, isol8 checks your entitlement, pulls that exact artifact, unpacks it into a fresh agent folder in your EFS workspace, and merges its config into your pod — remapping IDs so it slots in cleanly next to your other agents.
in the stack · S3 artifact (workspace.tar.gz + manifest.json + openclaw-slice.json), deployed via catalog_service

11Models & inference

Which AI brain powers your agents — and who pays for it.

You choose how inference is billed and routed: managed Bedrock (drawn from your isol8 credits), signing in with your own ChatGPT account (OAuth, personal owners only), or bringing your own Anthropic / OpenAI API key (encrypted at rest).

Whatever you pick, you still set what the agents can reach. The scope is yours, not the model’s.

12Channels

Reaching your agents where you already talk.

You can message your agents over Slack, Discord, WhatsApp, or Telegram — like texting a teammate. Because your pod is always on, they respond and keep working even when you’re away from your desk.
