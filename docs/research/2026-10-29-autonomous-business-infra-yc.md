## 1. What YC's Request for Startups says

- The live [ycombinator.com/rfs](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs) renders only **Winter 2027** (11 requests) and contains none of the phrases "autonomous company", "agent-run business", "AI employees", "AI operating system", "software for agents" or "cloud for small software" [P]. Nearest lines: "AI that turns an ordinary worker into an expert" (Brad Flora, *The $15-an-Hour Expert*); "many will also own specialized models that encode how their business works" (Jon Xu, *Own Your Intelligence*); "soon AI agents will handle enrollment" (Gustaf Alströmer) [P].
- Older editions do not load headlessly. Their text below is from the Modelence mirror, which says it "created detailed, AI-ready prompts for each one", so treat the wording as a rewrite [S]:
  - **Summer 2026, "The AI Operating System for Companies", Diana Hu**: "shared infrastructure all company AI agents run on"; "unified, permissioned company knowledge graph"; "Enforce governance, compliance, and usage policies" [S](https://modelence.com/yc-rfs-summer-2026/the-ai-operating-system-for-companies).
  - **Summer 2026, "Company Brain", Tom Blomfield**: "institutional memory and intelligence layer for an entire company" [S](https://modelence.com/yc-rfs-summer-2026/company-brain).
  - **Summer 2026, "Software for Agents", Aaron Epstein**: "makes AI agents reliable, observable, and safe"; demo list includes "a safety approval gate", two-agent messaging, cross-session memory [S](https://modelence.com/yc-rfs-summer-2026/software-for-agents); another mirror: "machine-readable interfaces (APIs, MCPs, CLIs)", "instant setup without human in the loop" [S](https://dev.to/t49qnsx7qtkpanks/ycs-summer-2026-theme-is-make-something-agents-want-heres-what-that-means-for-the-payment-2lec). Nothing on agents owning accounts or money.
  - **Summer 2026, "AI-Native Service Companies", Alströmer**: "AI agents as the primary workforce"; **Fall 2026, "A Cloud for Small Software", Pete Koomen**: "from code to a shared, secured URL in one step" (both yc-bets note) [S].
  - **Fall 2026, "Multiplayer AI", Aaron Epstein**: "Today AI sessions are single-player"; "shared team experiences that anyone can watch, redirect, or take over"; "Keep a shared history of what agents did and why" [S](https://modelence.com/yc-rfs-fall-2026/multiplayer-ai).
- Read: YC never asked for "autonomous companies"; that phrase is Naïve's and Pazi's. YC asked for the parts: shared agent infrastructure with governance (Hu), approval gates (Epstein), a cloud for one-person software (Koomen), a team watching agents together (Epstein). The last two describe a Lightbulb room with Canvas better than they describe Naïve.

| **Agentic Fabriq** | `afctl`, Python SDK, one MCP endpoint, gateway, console [P] | Not stated | Money, email, phone, compute, hosting; approval holds and per-user permissions below Enterprise; it governs tool calls, it does not run agents |

Pattern: every whole-stack "autonomous company" seller (Naïve, Pazi, The Company Company) hosts on its own accounts and sells a prepaid balance; every single-leg seller (mail, phone, cards, identity) is an API with usage pricing and is already, or could be, a Stripe Projects provider. Nobody combines customer-owned accounts with a team surface.

Sceptical summary. Lightbulb does not "become" Naïve by adding legs: Naïve has $28.5M, twelve people and the same substrate, and the YC whole-stack peers behind it are a consumer dashboard (Pazi) and a one-person pre-product. What no YC company has is customer-owned accounts through Stripe Projects plus a team surface where the room is the permission and the history; that maps to Koomen and Epstein's Multiplayer AI, not to "autonomous companies". The three gaps that would stop a serious customer, in order: credentials in every agent's `.env`; no agent money beyond catalog spend caps; the Apps and infrastructure leg unproven in production.

## 5. What this means for the Apps design

1. Name the thing by YC's words that fit: "a shared, secured URL in one step" and "shared history of what agents did and why". Not "autonomous company infrastructure"; YC never wrote that and Naïve owns it.
2. Customer-owned accounts is the one differentiator no YC company in this note has. Keep it, and prove it in production on one real services account before anything else.
3. Add `agentmail/inbox` and `agentphone/number` to the `services:` vocabulary; they are catalog names already, so an app or its builder gets an inbox or number through a card, not a build.
4. Move every provider credential, not only app credentials, into Secrets Manager under its resource; `.env` on every roster agent is the gap Agentic Fabriq and Naïve both sell against.
5. Make `budget_usd` the Naïve rule: a hard cap the builder cannot raise, enforced by the per-provider Projects limit and a Paperclip budget on the builder.
6. Give each app an MCP endpoint (Naïve's pattern) so other agents in the workspace get the app's tools; the starter already has server routes.
7. When a build outgrows the 30-minute turn, provision an E2B or Daytona sandbox through the rail on the customer's account; Naïve's computers are on Naïve's account, so this is a second differentiator at no design cost.
8. Do not add cards or wallets. Agentcard's $5,000/month floor says agent money is enterprise-priced and regulated; the Projects spend cap is the honest answer until a customer asks for more.
9. Surface Paperclip's budgets, hiring approvals and audit log in the room; YC's "AI OS for companies" asks for exactly that governance and the substrate already has it.
10. The measure is Koomen's: minutes from "build me" in a room to a second person opening it in Canvas, with no account created. Nothing else in this note is a metric.
