## 0. The answer in brief

1. **We may not reverse-engineer Lovable.** Its Terms forbid reverse engineering, attempts to work out "system logic", and automated access, with no exception for what local law allows (§1). I did no reverse engineering, downloaded no bundle and captured no network traffic. Everything below comes from public docs, the changelog, the blog and Lovable's own open-source repos. Those cover almost every question we would have asked a bundle.
2. **The first Dyad read missed four Apache-licensed parts worth having:** a **flow recorder** that turns clicks in the preview into Playwright tests (with fragile-selector repair); the benchmark's **oracle and broken-twin controls** and multi-persona **security probes**; a **supply-chain install policy** (a 1-day minimum package age plus the Socket firewall); and a **compaction prompt** that carries the user's standing preferences forward. It also wrongly listed the MCP-consent prompt as FSL: the prompt text is Apache (§2).
3. **Dyad cannot teach us where Lovable is ahead.** Dyad has no payments, no design system or design-scan, no hosted preview, no collaboration and no backups. Its "smart context" runs on Dyad's closed server, not in the repo (§2).
4. **Ten gaps in our design matter most** (§3): first-preview speed; design quality; visual edits; saved flow tests; a security scan and safe auto-fix at publish; stuck-run detection that uses a knowledge base, not only a fix cap; code-only undo without data snapshots on preview; a shared draft and Accept flow when two people edit one app; import (from a URL, GitHub or a Lovable export); and cost shown while the builder works.
5. Revised §7 text and a "match or beat" list are in §4.

## 1. Legal first: can we reverse-engineer Lovable?

### What the Terms say

Source: https://lovable.dev/terms, "Last Updated: August 28, 2026" [P]. Read through a fetch tool on 2026-10-29. Each quote is under 15 words, and **counsel should check them against the live page before relying on them.**

Under **License Restrictions**, introduced by "You agree not to, and not to allow others to:":

| # | Clause (verbatim, trimmed) | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "reverse engineer, decompile, disassemble, or otherwise attempt to derive source code, models, or algorithms" | Inspecting the client bundle to recover how the editor or agent works |
| 2 | "extract, reconstruct, or attempt to determine model weights, training data, or system logic" | Working out the agent's prompts, tools or routing from network traffic |
| 3 | "use automated tools (such as bots, scrapers, or crawlers) to access… the Services" | Any agent-driven browsing, capture or scraping of lovable.dev |
| 4 | "copy, modify, distribute, sell, lease, sublicense, or otherwise exploit the Services or Lovable Materials" | Lifting their UI, copy or assets |
| 5 | "use the Services, AI Output, or prompts to train, develop, or improve competing AI models" | Training or tuning our models on Lovable output |

The only general carve-out in that list is "except as expressly permitted in these Terms". The reverse-engineering ban has **no exception for applicable law** [P].

The Terms bring in the Platform Rules (https://lovable.dev/platform-rules, "Last Updated: February 2026") [P]. Relevant lines:
- §11 Security: "Trying to bypass security measures or platform safeguards."
- §14 Copyright and Trademark: "Publishing or sharing one-to-one copies of existing commercial or known sites and digital services". That rule is about what users publish on Lovable. It also means a rebuilt look-alike of Lovable would be treated as "malicious".

Two clauses run the other way, toward Lovable's own use of data: it may use Usage Data for "monitoring, analytics, benchmarking" and Customer Data for "creating benchmarks" [P]. Anything we type into Lovable while trying it out is therefore Lovable's data to learn from.

### Verdict

**Not permitted.** Inspecting Lovable's client bundle or network calls to learn how it works breaks clauses 1 and 2. Doing it with an agent or the REA tools also breaks clause 3. This is the terms read plainly, not legal advice. My own call: even where a court in some jurisdiction would not enforce the clause, a Lovable account holder who does this is in breach, and the downside (a contract claim, a ban, a bad story) is far larger than what we would learn. **I did not inspect Lovable's bundle or traffic, and the REA tools were not pointed at Lovable.**

Manual use of the product as a customer (signing up, building something, reading what the screen shows) is ordinary use, and none of the clauses above forbids it on its face. Keep it human: no automated sessions, no capture tools, no prompts aimed at extracting the system prompt ("system logic"). Use a throwaway project with no company data, because of the benchmarking clause. Have counsel confirm before the team leans on hands-on sessions.

## 4. Recommendation

### 4.1 Revised §7 text, "What we take from Dyad"

> **7. What we take from Dyad, and what we take from Lovable's public record**
>
> Dyad's agent loop and every tool sit in `src/pro` (FSL, a two-year competing-use ban). We take nothing from there, and our builders bring their own loop. From Dyad's Apache code (commit `0ee4a524`), with a file header, the Apache licence text and a NOTICE line:
> - **Starter:** its Next.js template (App Router, shadcn, Tailwind) with its stack defaults (react-hook-form + zod, sonner, recharts, lucide), plus our Drizzle, door, error reporter and design tokens.
> - **Rules for `AGENTS.md` and the skill:** simplicity, completeness, understand-plan-implement-verify, fix-loop rules and blueprint-first from its prompts; the Next.js database rules from `neon_prompt.ts` (where `DATABASE_URL` may live, server-side authorization rather than row-level security, empty-database first run); per-switch sections added by `up`, using its guide-filter pattern.
> - **`lightbulb app check`:** its type-check parser (ported to Rust); its schema classifier as the destructive-migration gate; its install policy (a minimum package age of one day, approved build scripts only); bounded output on every log or error read.
> - **Versioning:** one commit per turn; undo as a new commit; "data restored" only when it was.
> - **Errors:** its in-page error capture, posting to the app's own route in bounded batches.
> - **Tests:** its flow recorder's code generator and selector rules, so the done-gate browser pass is saved as a Playwright test and fragile selectors become `data-testid`s.
> - **Visual pointing:** its component tagger in preview builds, so a click in Canvas tells the builder the file and line.
> - **Review:** its security-review prompt (grounded findings, severities, plain language) and the allow/ask/block consent policy, for the reviewer and for deciding when the builder needs an owner card.
> - **State:** its compaction shape, including standing preferences, for our state files.
> - **Evals:** its app-builder benchmark: milestone specs, fixed journeys, multi-persona security probes, an LLM judge, and **an oracle app that must score 100% and a broken twin that must fail every probe before any run counts**.
>
> We do not take its runtime, preview iframe, state machines, Supabase or Cloudflare flows, smart context (it runs on Dyad's server), or theme prompt.
>
> **Lovable is a reference, never a source.** Its Terms forbid reverse engineering and automated access. We learn only from its docs, changelog, blog and its open repositories (`lovablelabs/oj`, `mcp`, `lovable-cursor-plugin`). We copy no code or text from leaked prompts.

### 4.2 What Lovable does better that we must match or beat

Ordered by how much a room would notice. Each has the mechanism we would build.

1. **Seeing something fast.** *Match:* the first preview never waits on database provisioning. A frontend-only preview with sample data comes first, while Neon provisions in parallel; progress lines come from the run log; there is a 5-minute target in Stage 0. *Beat:* the preview arrives with a recording and the person's own brand and data already in it.
2. **A good look by default.** *Match:* design tokens in the starter, the one design card, and a token lint in `check` that fails on raw colours, arbitrary values and duplicate base components (Lovable's scan, made deterministic and free). *Beat:* the brand is read from the workspace, so most companies get no card at all.
3. **Pointing at the thing.** *Match:* tagged preview builds; a click in Canvas attaches file, line and component to the room message. *Later:* direct text and class edits without the model.
4. **Proof that it works.** *Match:* a test for every business-rule change; the done-gate browser pass saved as a Playwright test. *Beat:* the reviewer runs two-persona security probes before every ship-it, and the recording goes in the room. Lovable's browser test is opt-in and hidden.
5. **Safe publish.** *Match:* dependency audit, minimum package age, a route-auth check, and `.lightbulb/security.md` for accepted risks; auto-fix only dependency bumps. *Beat:* findings go as cards to the owner, not to whoever pressed Publish.
6. **Getting unstuck.** *Match:* the existing cap and diagnosis step, plus `report-tool` (one per turn) and a curated fixes file built from the weekly lesson clusters, pruned by A/B. *Beat:* lessons are written by the builder automatically and recalled from Lightbulb memory. Lovable's carry over only when a human pastes them.
7. **Plans for big changes.** *Match:* a plan message with Build it / Change it for new data models, payments or roles, kept in `.lightbulb/plan.md`. *Beat:* the plan goes to the room, so the person who pays and the person who asked can both see it.
8. **Data safety.** *Match:* 14 days of daily live-database dumps, besides the dumps at publish; data restore stays a separate owner action. Lovable keeps daily backups for about 14 days; we cannot branch Neon without an admin key.
9. **Payments that fit the business.** *Match:* a test banner in preview, a product card for prices, live keys only at publish. *Beat:* ask whether the company already sells on Shopify (or another platform) and link to it rather than rebuilding checkout.
10. **Cost in view.** *Match:* cost in the done line, and a pause card when a turn passes the app's budget. *Beat:* it is the company's own spend on its own accounts, shown per app in Canvas.
11. **Starting from what exists.** *Match:* import from a URL, after an ownership question, and from a Lovable or Bolt export (ZIP or GitHub), adapted to the starter or refused with a reason. *Beat:* remix within the workspace ("one like the Leeds schedule") with no data or secrets copied.
12. **Long work.** *Match:* resume from `progress.md` plus git, tested in the eval by killing the builder halfway through a build. Lovable's 10-hour runs come from an event log; ours come from files and turns, and the eval must prove resume works.

Where we already beat Lovable and should keep it that way: the room as sign-in (Lovable has six sharing modes); approvals routed to the right person; mobile (Lovable has none); the customer owns every account; and an agent that keeps the app working after launch, not a daily email.
