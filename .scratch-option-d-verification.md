## 1. Voices

**The built-in list (gpt-live-1) has 22 names.** [doc] REF:159-211 gives `audio.output.voice` as an enum:

> alloy, ash, ballad, beacon, bossa, cedar, cinder, coral, delta, echo, gleam, marin, meridian, quartz, ripple, sage, shimmer, stone, tempo, verse, vesper, willow

It may also be a `CustomVoice {id}`. The default is `marin` (REF:161). OAI:live-conversations:28-46 lists 12 "additional" GPT-Live voices with accent and gender:

- quartz, ripple, vesper, willow, stone, gleam, meridian
- bossa and tempo (Portuguese)
- beacon, delta, cinder

"Additional" means on top of the realtime set, which the reference enum spells out.

- **Older realtime voices (alloy, ash, ballad, coral, echo, sage, shimmer, verse, cedar) are accepted.** [doc] All nine are in the reference enum. `fable`, `onyx` and `nova` are not; they are TTS-only names (OAI:text-to-speech:218). [inf] Accepted on paper. The guide only showcases the 12 new ones plus marin, so test that the older ones sound good on this model.
- **The plugin's `GPTLiveVoices` disagrees with OpenAI.** [src] It lists `aster, beacon, cinder, marin, stone, vesper` (LK GL:74; identical on upstream main, last touched #7401 2026-09-22). **`aster` is in no OpenAI document.** [inf] It is probably an alpha-era name (GL:46 says "measured on the alpha"). The type also accepts any `str` (GL:158), so the Literal is only a hint. Don't use `aster`. Choose from the 22.
- **Hermes's list has 14 names** (prior research §2) and is a subset of the 22.
- **Distinctness for a 6-agent roster.** [inf] There are enough English voices to give the six starter roles six clearly different voices: about 7 masculine and 6 feminine, plus marin, cedar and the classic set. Map `voice_for(agent_id)` over the 20 English names.

**Custom voices exist but cannot be per-agent at scale.** [doc] OAI:custom-voices:
- :11-14: "limited to eligible customers. Contact our sales team".
- :27-28: needs a consent recording **by the voice actor** plus a sample that must match it.
- :42: **at most 20 voices per organization**.
- :43, :174-177: samples ≤30 s, with at least 5 s of real speech and 15 tokens; 10–30 s is recommended; uploads ≤10 MiB.
- :159-162: needs a project-scoped key with `api.voices.read`/`write`.
- :190-193: gpt-live-1 supports custom voices "with English accents" only, and the accent must also be stated in instructions.
- :213-216: chosen at session creation; a revoked voice shows up as a 404.

So custom voices could give a handful of brand voices, never one per customer agent.

## 2. Session startup latency

**Documented:** no number. [doc]
- The protocol is: connect, send `session.start` (WebSocket) or `POST /v1/live/sessions` with SDP (WebRTC), then **wait for `session.started` before any audio or command** (OAI:voice-websockets?api=live:19; OAI:voice-webrtc?api=live:31).
- OpenAI offers "WARP" to cut WebRTC setup time (voice-webrtc:617-621), which implies setup time is noticeable.
- Docs advise that when restarting after an idle close, you should "buffer the opening speech through connection setup" (OAI:live-conversations:500).

**What the LiveKit plugin does at start.** [src]
1. `GPTLiveSession.__init__` starts `_main_task` at once (GL:428). That opens a **WebSocket** to `/v1/live/sessions` (GL:570-597, `ws_connect`; not WebRTC). It reports the connect time as a `RealtimeModelMetrics` event (GL:592, DX:211).
2. In parallel, the activity calls `_update_session(instructions, chat_ctx, tools)` (AA:1261-1267). That sets `_configured` (DX:191-209).
3. `_send_task` waits on `_configured`, then sends **one** `session.start` holding instructions, voice and the whole history (`input`) (GL:616-627, built at GL:455-492). There is no separate history upload.
4. Every later event, including audio, is held until `session.started` arrives (GL:629-633). Audio pushed before then is queued, not lost (`push_audio` → `send_event` → channel, GL:1038-1058).

The start cost is therefore one WebSocket handshake plus one `session.start` → `session.started` round trip on the server.

**Public measurements.** [doc, third party]
- **SIP report.** Community thread "GPT-Live greeting delayed and often glitched (SIP)", 2026-09-25 (https://community.openai.com/t/gpt-live-greeting-delayed-and-often-glitched-sip/1398920): "always a 2–3s silence" before the first greeting audio. gpt-realtime spoke within 1 s. In about half of calls the greeting was cut off or garbled. No staff reply.
- **Agora.** https://www.agora.io/en/blog/openai-didnt-publish-gpt-lives-latency-so-we-measured-it/ measured **turn** latency only: about 1.1 s median, with a tight 104 ms P90 spread. It has no startup numbers.
- No LiveKit issue reports startup time. Open GPT-Live issues: #7513 (no jitter prebuffer, audio breaks), #7551, #7552.

**Verdict:** [unk] There is no measured WebSocket `session.start` → `session.started` → first-audio figure. The best public evidence (SIP) suggests **about 2–3 s from connect to first speech** when the model speaks first. **Measure it before committing to D.** In the worker, log the time from `start_live` to the `session_started` event and to the first `audio_stream` frame, over about 20 runs.

**A design point this exposes.** [inf] In D the routing utterance is heard by **our** STT before the session exists, so the new GPT-Live session never hears the request. It must be handed over as text:
- the last seeded `user` message plus `generate_reply()`. Today that is `reply_to`, which sends commentary "Reply to the caller now…" (GL:1183-1205; W:69-70). Our own comment calls it "a request the model may decline" (W:69).
- `session.delegation.created` will **not** fire for words the model did not hear. So the first turn's Hermes delegation must be started by us, client-side, at routing time, not by the model. Its results then go back as `append_thinking` / `append_commentary(delegation_id=None)`. A null id is allowed: OAI:live-delegation and LK GL:1060-1076.

## 3. Seeding history

- **Supported at start: [doc]**
  - `session.input` is "Ordered text-only history supplied before startup … developer, user, and assistant messages with one text part each; at most 128 messages and 8,192 rendered tokens in total" (REF:427-429; OAI:live-conversations:97).
  - Developer and user messages use `input_text`; assistant messages use `output_text`.
  - It is immutable after start (OAI:live-conversations:59). Later context only goes in through `instructions/thinking/commentary.append`, each ≤500 tokens.
- **The LiveKit mechanism: [src]**
  - Pass the history as **`Agent(chat_ctx=…)`** (LK `agents/voice/agent.py:53`). On activity start, `_update_session(chat_ctx=self._agent.chat_ctx)` (AA:1265) reaches `GPTLiveSession._append_items`, which stores the items while `session.start` is unsent (GL:1131-1134). `_session_start_event` renders them into `input` (GL:455-492).
  - **`AgentSession.start()` has no `chat_ctx` parameter** (AS:892-904). The Agent's `chat_ctx` is the only way in.
  - `update_chat_ctx` **after** start does not add history. New items are folded into **one `session.thinking.append`** as `role: text` lines (GL:1150-1154; DA:574-596 warns "duplex context is append-only"). System and developer messages become `append_instructions` (GL:1142-1144).
- **Limits in the plugin: [src]**
  - It caps **items at 128, keeping the newest** and logging a warning (GL:44, :457-478).
  - It does **not** count tokens: "the service also caps startup history at 8192 tokens … there is no tokenizer here" (GL:42-43). [unk] Whether too many tokens is rejected at start or truncated is undocumented. **We must trim to about 8k tokens ourselves.** Hermes uses 6,000 chars total and 1,200 per message, which is safe.
  - Roles: `system`→`developer`, and `user`/`assistant` pass through. Tool calls and results are narrated as `developer` lines (GL:1257-1268). Only `text_content` is kept: **text only**, so audio and images are dropped.
- **Several named speakers: [inf, undocumented]** The schema has three roles and no speaker or name field (REF:427-440). The only way is a **text prefix inside the content**: `user` "Prasiddha: …". For **another agent's** lines (Gauge), use `user` or `developer` with a prefix such as "Gauge (teammate agent): …". **Never `assistant`.** `assistant` means "what I, this voice, said earlier", and that is exactly the identity bleed D exists to fix. Only this agent's own past lines should be `assistant`.
  - [unk] Risk: the model may echo the `Name:` style in speech. Add one instruction line: "Lines prefixed with a name are other people; never prefix your own speech". Test it.

## 4. Billing

- **The rate. [doc]** "$0.05 per minute, billed per second … not rounded up" (MODEL:23-31; pricing.md:271-279).
- **What is billed. [doc]** OAI:voice-latency-cost:20-26: "Active session time includes time when the user speaks, the assistant speaks, both are silent, or the backend is working … count the active session from start through closure … Muting microphone input does not close the session." So it is **wall-clock session time, idle included**. Backend (Responses) usage is billed separately; under client delegation (ours) there is none from OpenAI. Usage is reported as cumulative `usage.seconds` (LK GT:290-311; OAI:live-conversations:540-551).
- **The 15-second charge.** The exact wording [doc] (OAI:voice-latency-cost:30-34; also OAI:voice-webrtc?api=live:35):

  > "A `POST /v1/live/sessions` request to create a **WebRTC** session bills 15 seconds of voice duration while the session initializes. That amount is credited against duration charges once the session starts running. Don't add another 15 seconds … Account for session-creation charges when evaluating reconnects or applications that create sessions before the user is ready to speak."

  - It is **not** an extra 15 s per session. It is a **pre-charge** that is credited, so in effect it is **a 15 s minimum for a WebRTC session** [inf: a session shorter than 15 s still pays 15 s].
  - It is stated for **WebRTC only**. The LiveKit plugin connects over **WebSocket** (GL:589). [inf] The documented 15 s charge does not apply to us. With no stated minimum, a WebSocket session bills its actual seconds. [unk] This is not confirmed for WebSocket; check `usage.seconds` on a very short session.
- [unk] Whether the up-to-5-second close drain (`session.close` → `session.closed`, GL:62, :602-614) is billed is undocumented. Assume yes.

## 5. Fast switching

- **Concurrency. [doc]** MODEL:74-88: "Rate limits are measured in concurrent sessions". Tier 1 = 25, Tier 2 = 50, Tier 3 = 200, Tier 4 = 300, Tier 5 = 500; Free is unsupported. These are per org/tier limits, not per key, following OpenAI's usual model. [inf] Under D each active Live room or call holds **one** session (two briefly during a switch). At Tier 1, that means about 25 rooms speaking at once across all customers. Check our org's tier.
- **Pre-warming a full session (muted) works but costs the same as speaking.** [doc] Muting does not stop billing (voice-latency-cost:24-25; live-conversations:503). A pre-warmed second agent doubles the cost while warm: $0.05/min each. Docs explicitly tell you to weigh creation cost against "the delay before voice becomes ready" (live-conversations:503).
  - There is also a logic problem [inf]: persona, voice and history are frozen at `session.start`. So a pre-warmed session must be opened for a **specific** agent, with history that goes stale as the room talks. Late context can only be squeezed in as ≤500-token appends.
  - Pre-warming only works for a predicted next speaker. Example: in a DM it is always the same agent, so just keep that session open.
- **A cheaper partial pre-warm. [src + inf]** The plugin opens the WebSocket immediately and holds `session.start` until `_configured` is set (GL:428, :616-627). Billing counts "from start" (voice-latency-cost:23). [unk] Whether a connected socket with no `session.start` is billed, or how long the server keeps it open, is unknown. At best it saves the TCP/TLS/WebSocket handshake (tens to a few hundred ms), not the server's session spin-up.
- **Two `AgentSession`s in one job: allowed, but they share one identity.** [src]
  - AS:913-916 and :940-958: a second `AgentSession` in the same job is legal. It becomes **non-primary**, with recording off and no session host. There is no rule against it.
  - Both publish through `room.local_participant`, i.e. **one LiveKit identity** (W:26 `agent-ceo`), with `audio_track_name` per `RoomOptions` (room_io/types.py:285; _output.py:88-95). So two agents would be two **tracks** under one participant, told apart only by track name or attributes.
  - The `lk.agent.state` attribute is written to the local participant by each RoomIO (room_io.py:502-513), so two sessions overwrite each other's state.
  - The chat text-stream handler registers once; the second RoomIO logs and ignores it (room_io.py:99-103).
  - [inf] A real **separate participant per agent** needs a separate room connection, i.e. another job/dispatch or a manual `rtc.Room` with its own token. RoomIO does not do this.
- **Switching inside one `AgentSession` works through `session.update_agent(new Agent(llm=GPTLiveModel(voice=…), instructions=persona, chat_ctx=seed))`.** [src]
  - An `Agent` takes its own `llm` (agent.py:59).
  - A different llm instance means the realtime session is **not reused** (AA:1091-1094, which reuses only `self.llm is new_activity.llm`). Even with the same instance, reuse fails because instructions are immutable (AA:1109-1112, GL:201-202).
  - The order is strictly **serial**: old activity `drain()` → `aclose()` → `await rt_session.aclose()` (AS:1899-1900, AA:1596-1597). That close waits **up to 5 s for `session.closed`** (GL:62, :609-613). Only then does the new activity start, connect and wait for `session.started` (AS:1940-1941).
  - Worst case for a switch ≈ ≤5 s close drain + connect + startup. **Better: don't await the old close on the critical path.**
- **What we already have.** [src] Our worker already builds **a new `AgentSession` per summon** (`start_live`, W:89-101) and tears it down with `aclose()` plus an unpublish of the `roomio_audio` track (W:79-86). D fits this seam directly: `start_live(persona=agent's, voice=agent's, chat_ctx=seed)`.
  - To switch fast, start the new `start_live` **concurrently** with the old `_Live.aclose()`. This overlaps two sessions for ≤5 s, which is one extra concurrency slot and a few cents.
  - Give each agent's track its own `audio_track_name`, or set a participant attribute naming the speaking agent, so clients can label who is talking.

## 6. Persona / instruction size

- **The limit. [doc]** `instructions` takes "up to 16,384 tokens" (OAI:live-conversations:22; REF:577 "Limited to 16,384 client-supplied tokens … Immutable after startup").
- **Our persona fits easily. [inf]** A 1,500-char SOUL.md excerpt is about 400 tokens. A roster of about 6 agents plus about 10 humans with one-line cards is roughly 300–600 tokens; rules are about 300–500. The total is about 1.5k tokens, roughly 10% of the limit.
- **History sits outside that limit.** `input` has its own 8,192-token budget; the context window is 128k (live-conversations:306).
- [doc] The prompting guide recommends a short front-end prompt, with business rules on the backend (OAI:live:11-12; REF:577). So keep the SOUL excerpt to voice and personality, not procedures.
- Note [src]: the plugin rejects any later `update_instructions` that changes the text (GL:1107-1113).

## 7. Other things that make D harder or easier

1. **Delegation per session is fine. [src]** Each `GPTLiveSession` emits its own `delegation_created` (GL:720-721, :836-847). Our `_CEO.on_enter` binds to `self.duplex_session` (W:36-39), so a per-agent `Agent` subclass binds to its own session automatically. The delegation must still be routed to **that agent's** Hermes session key instead of `voice_routing` (backend).
2. **The first turn after a switch is ours, not the model's** (§2). The new session didn't hear the request. Either we delegate directly at routing time, or we seed the text and `generate_reply`. `generate_reply` is commentary, which the model may paraphrase or decline.
3. **Possible dead air and garbled openings on fresh sessions.** The SIP report gives 2–3 s of silence and cut-off greetings in about half of calls (§2). This is not yet seen on WebSocket. Mitigation [inf]:
   - our STT already has the words, so the client UI can show "Forge is joining" at once;
   - alternatively, play an earcon while the session starts.
4. **Voice change across a switch is the point of D**, and it is legitimate: a different person is speaking. [inf] What can feel unnatural:
   - The new agent has no memory of *how* things were said, only seeded text (text-only `input`, GL:1257-1268).
   - The new agent's first sentence is a cold start, with no backchannel continuity.
   - Seeding the previous agent's last lines as `user` "Gauge (agent): …" keeps the new agent from repeating them.
5. **Cost profile.** Per-agent sessions do **not** multiply cost when only one is open: still $0.05/min for the floor holder. Idle seconds while an agent holds the floor are billed. [inf] Closing a session when the floor drops costs a fresh startup (latency) the next time. In a DM, the same agent always holds the floor, so D for DMs is just "Option A" with no switching at all.
6. **The service closes sessions on its own.** It can end one with `expired` (an undocumented duration limit, live-conversations:570) or `content` (safety). The plugin's reconnect reseeds from its own `_history` with the **same** persona and voice (GL:510-568, :555-568), which is fine for D.
7. **`aster` in LiveKit's Literal is wrong.** It doesn't block anything, because any `str` passes (GL:158). [inf] It may be worth an upstream issue.
