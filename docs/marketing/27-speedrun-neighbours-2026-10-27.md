## Findings

1. **The memory-layer companies do not sell from a landing page; they sell paid pilots.** Infragrid's public site does not even describe the memory product. Contxt's site is one sentence and "Request a paid pilot". Sonder and Proper Motion are manifestos with "Book a demo". Their ARR claims ($302K in two weeks, $270K in three) come from founder-led pilots. This is 01-marketing-plan's "never cut" list restated by six other companies: the founder-led setups are the stage, and the page's job is to make the pilot ask credible (named customers, prices, security), not to sell the memory story on its own. (marketing-plan, cro)
2. **Every headline that exists names the reader's outcome in plain verbs, and the category noun is infrastructure, never "employees".** "Skip the cold start." "See exactly how agents experience your product." "Get the job you want done." "The network where coding agents learn from each other." Lightbulb's noun is messaging; the 26 Oct ruling holds and the page keeps saying it in concrete words (people talk to people, people talk to agents, agents talk to agents). (product-marketing, copywriting)
3. **Shared memory: four ways to build it, and Lightbulb gets it as a byproduct.** Contxt and Codos build it by integration (a "memory and action layer", a "company brain" deployed in step 2 of a services engagement). Space builds it as a drive. Sonder and Infragrid build it by watching screens, which collides with the trust story a developer reads before installing a daemon (26, Finding 9). Lightbulb is the room: every message, handoff and approval is already the shared record, and the network already lets an agent read the rooms it is in, under the team's permissions. The plan should say so in §6: the record is the memory; agents from any vendor read it; there is no separate memory product and no screen-watching. This is the game-theory point of workstream 26 in product terms: the neutral room is the one place the record accumulates for every vendor's agent. (product-marketing)
4. **Recommending automations needs the record first, so it is a Q1 feature and not a homepage claim.** Infragrid recommends agents from clicks and logs; Alia flags "conflicting decisions, unclear ownership, and cross-team dependencies" from tools and posts in Slack naming who can unblock. The Lightbulb version is cheaper and more private: the repeated handoff in a room is the automation candidate ("this went to Finance four times this week; make it a standing task"). It goes on the page only when a customer has used it (the plan's rule against fabricated proof). (product-marketing, cro)
5. **Saving money at our size is seat-free pricing and work approved in the room, not an ROI number.** Codos sells "10x+ ROI" to 300–3,000-person companies through a diagnostics → pilot → transformation ladder; Bounty sells "pay when it's done" and had to build escrow and an automated check to make it safe. For a 3–15 person team the honest line is: agents do not pay seats, and the approval in the room is the verification step. Keep "30 days free, no card". Claim an ROI only when a customer states one. (pricing, copy-editing)
6. **ChatOverflow is the network's nearest neighbour and a different product.** Its agents learn from strangers' agents (search before every task, post what it figured out, vote); ours talk to the team's own agents, privately. Do not drift toward "agents learn from each other". Borrow its one good pattern: a privacy-steps section on /network and /security that says in four steps what leaves the machine and when a person approves it. Its proof counters at zero are the lesson in the other direction: ship the number or leave the counter out. (competitors, copywriting)
7. **Alia is the workspace's nearest neighbour and lives inside Slack as a guest.** Same customer language as ours ("coordination is now the bottleneck" / "the first thing that broke was adding the cofounder"). The difference the page states without naming anyone: in a chat tool the agent is a guest that posts; in Lightbulb it is a member with a session, a room and an approval. The spike hero already shows that (the "Jonah's Codex" session tag, "Approve both"). (competitors)
8. **Page mechanics worth taking.** AgentCat: two doors ("Start for free" + "Request a demo"), "$0 · forever free · no card", quantified named testimonials, a security row (open source, self-hosting) that reads as "enterprise-ready from day one". These match the spike's two buttons, the trust row and /security. Space's "for you, your team, and your agents" includes the solo person who arrives through the network door; it is a candidate cadence for the H1. Proof: everyone else shows "Backed by a16z speedrun" and nothing; we show named small-business customers, which is stronger for a founder-led buyer. Keep it. (cro, copywriting)
9. **The investor sentence is a separate artefact from the page, and the plan should have one.** Each company carries a noun plus an ARR-velocity line for investors and a verb-led outcome for buyers. Lightbulb's noun is "messaging for people and their AI agents"; its velocity line is the number on the wall (ten teams with a card by 4 January). Put the sentence in §1 of the plan for fundraise-prep; never on the site. (product-marketing)

## Recommendation

Keep the messaging headline and the two doors; add nothing about memory or automations to the page until a customer has used them. In the business plan, make the record the moat in words: the room is the memory, recommendations come from the record, and money is saved by seat-free pricing and approvals rather than by an ROI promise. Founder-led pilots stay the stage, as they are for every neighbour here.

Alternatives considered and rejected:

- **Headline on the memory or automation story** ("Your team's agents remember everything" or "Automations recommended from your chat"). Nobody can try it today; it fails the no-fabricated-proof rule and moves the page off messaging again. Rejected.
- **A global knowledge network between agents** (the ChatOverflow shape). Different product, different trust model (strangers), and the free layer is already a race to zero (26). Rejected.
- **Screen-watching for memory or recommendations** (the Sonder and Infragrid shape). Contradicts the end-to-end, nothing-leaves-your-machine story the network is sold on. Rejected.
- **"Pay when it's done" pricing** (the Bounty shape). Needs escrow and automated verification; our approval already does the job inside the room for a team that trusts its own agents. Revisit only if a marketplace of outside agents is ever on the roadmap.
