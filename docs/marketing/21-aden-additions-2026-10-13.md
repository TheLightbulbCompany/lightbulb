Hacker News and Reddit

A Show HN is suitable only when readers can use the real product. Hacker News excludes landing pages, signup pages, and projects that are not ready to try. The title must begin with "Show HN:". The maker should be available to discuss the product and must not ask people to vote or comment. Hacker News also says not to use generated or AI-edited text in submissions or comments. The founder must therefore write the final title, submission, and replies personally. A useful structure is: the problem personally observed, one real workflow, what was built, an honest design choice or limitation, a direct path to try it, and one specific request for feedback.

Reddit needs a separate useful post for each community. Check the live rules on the day of posting. r/SideProject may fit a transparent builder update. r/SaaS is conditional and should use only a clearly permitted format. r/startups routes promotion to its recurring Share Your Startup thread. r/Entrepreneur is not suitable for a launch post. Disclose the founder relationship, ask one useful question, link only when permitted, and never coordinate votes or repeat the same copy across communities.

Follow-up emails

Every customer should know what happened, what they can do next, and who owns the next step. A follow-up is useful only when it removes uncertainty or helps the customer complete the next action.

Timing

Support: stay in the same thread. If waiting for the customer, send one reminder after two business days and a final note after five business days.
Work we owe: update the customer by the promised time even when the fix is not ready. Check once on the next business day after a fix.
Billing: when Stripe shows a failed payment, explain the current state and safe next step. Send one reminder after seven days only if it remains unresolved.
Founding teams: use the shared Lightbulb channel for the Thursday check-in. Use email only when the team has not been present there during the week.

Short sequence

Outcome: say what the customer can do now, or what prevents it.
Next step: give one action, one link, or one question.
Ownership: name who is handling it and when the next update will arrive.
Close the loop: confirm the result or explain when contact will resume.

Stop when the customer confirms the result, the current record shows resolution, the customer asks us to stop, the address bounces or is suppressed, or the message would repeat one already sent. Do not silently close an unresolved thread.

16. Competition/Hackathon
UMiami hackathon pilot: prove value before repeating it

A student team should leave with a useful result, not just a prize entry. For example, four students could use one Lightbulb workspace to research a campus problem, divide the work, review each other's output, and prepare a final demo without losing the shared context.

The possible prize pool is up to $1,000, subject to founder approval. For planning only, assume $0 to $500 for other direct costs if UMiami supplies the venue and basic equipment. This gives an illustrative cash range of $1,000 to $1,500. Other costs may include food, travel, printing, insurance or event fees, payment fees, and product usage. Also plan for 12 to 20 founder hours.

Use 12 confirmed active participants, likely four teams of three, as the proposed go or no-go floor. If fewer than 12 are confirmed by the chosen cutoff, reduce or postpone the pilot rather than spend the full prize pool.

Record registrations, attendance, active participants, completed demos, cash spent, and founder hours. For each team, record whether it starts a trial workspace, activates within seven days, and returns on day 28. An activated workspace has at least five human messages across at least two days and at least one agent reply. Also record what work the team completed, one concrete example of coordination saved, and whether a named university contact asks for a follow-up.

Proposed success means at least 12 participants, four completed demos, three activated team workspaces within seven days, two active at day 28, and one university contact requesting a next conversation. Fewer than eight participants, fewer than two completed demos, fewer than two activated workspaces, or no use after the event means this format failed.

Repeat only when full economic cost divided by activated workspaces is at or below a founder-approved acquisition ceiling. If paid conversion and retention are not known, do not call the pilot profitable. Confirm the final prize, payment terms, event format, university support, cost caps, time value, thresholds, and acquisition ceiling before making any commitment.

18. Long form content

Long-form content should teach one useful workflow at a time. Each piece starts with a real situation, shows the work step by step, says what did not work, and ends with a practical checklist. Do not write a broad essay about autonomous agents.

How a small team stops rebuilding AI context. A founder shares a customer brief, an agent researches one question, and a teammate reviews and continues the work in one room.
A practical handoff between a person and an AI agent. A teammate starts a coding task, shares the session and files, and another teammate reviews the work without asking for the project history again.
What we learned while setting up the first team workspaces. Show the first room, shared brief, agent request, teammate review, what confused the team, and what Lightbulb still needs to improve.

Each article needs founder-approved evidence, current screenshots, and private details removed. Do not invent a customer result or imply time savings that were not measured.
